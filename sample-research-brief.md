## The big picture
Scamwatch says small businesses are often targeted through fake investment opportunities, invoices and ads, and that scammers also impersonate real small businesses to defraud them and their customers. It also says small businesses lose more money to investment scams than any other type of scam.
Source: https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/stop-check-protect/protect-your-small-business-from-scams

## Three scams to know
**1. Payment redirection (business email compromise).** A scammer pretends to be a business you use and sends an invoice with new payee information, hoping you redirect a real payment to them. Scamwatch also describes requests to transfer a large sum out of the business, claiming to be from the owner or senior staff.
*Warning signs (Scamwatch):* bills for things you didn't order; a supplier's payment details change without notice; an email address or website with a spelling error or that is slightly different to what you normally see. (Scamwatch's protect steps add that scammers sometimes add one extra letter or number to an email address.)
*What to do:* stop and check any change to payee details; phone the business using a number you found independently.
Sources: https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/types-of-scams/business-email-compromise-scams ; https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/stop-check-protect/protect-your-small-business-from-scams

**2. Fake or unordered bills and buying scams.** Scammers request payment for directory listings, advertising and domain-name renewals you didn't order, or offer low prices on things like bulk office supplies and cleaning products using fake websites, ads, reviews and profiles on real retailer sites.
Source: https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/stop-check-protect/protect-your-small-business-from-scams

**3. Fake investments.** Scammers build fake investment opportunities with websites, ads and financial documents, and fake trading platforms that look and work like real apps, with fake market data and profit growth.
Source: https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/stop-check-protect/protect-your-small-business-from-scams

## If you think you've been scammed: the first hour
Scamwatch's order, in short:
1. **Contact your bank or card provider immediately** to report the scam and ask them to stop any transactions. If you're unsure, stop sending money; scammers keep asking until you stop.
2. **Get help to recover.** IDCARE, the national identity and cyber support service, can help you make a free plan. Scamwatch lists 1800 595 160 for IDCARE (IDCARE's own small-business page lists a different number, 1800 595 170, below, so confirm the number on idcare.org before calling).
3. **Warn others and report.** Report to Scamwatch, and you can make an official police report online.
4. **Watch for follow-up scams.** Scamwatch says 1 in 3 victims are scammed more than once, especially by anyone offering to get your money back.
Source: https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/protect-yourself/what-to-do-if-youve-been-scammed

If you're unhappy with how your bank responded, Scamwatch points to the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA). AFCA's homepage says it will operate the Australian Scams Ombudsman from 31 March 2027. Source: https://www.afca.org.au/

## Free help for small businesses
IDCARE's federally funded Small Business Cyber Resilience Service is free for businesses with 19 or fewer full-time equivalent employees (excluding the owner) that are registered in Australia, actively trading and have a valid ABN. It starts with a Cyber Health Check, then a free advisory session. IDCARE's small business page lists 1800 595 170.
Source: https://www.idcare.org/smallbusiness

Cybercrime reporting: the ASD's cyber.gov.au has a "Report" page with a "Report a cybercrime" option (it says to call 000 if there is an immediate threat to life or risk of harm). Source: https://www.cyber.gov.au/report-and-recover/report
